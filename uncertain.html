What we are not sure about

This site is built from careful research, but not every fact is certain. Below is everything we marked as uncertain. If you know more, or can check an archive, please tell us.

Facts to check

The research agents marked these as uncertain. A historian should check them against the HLS or the Historischer Atlas der Schweiz.

Dates

  • UR, SZ, Unterwalden: HLS says the alliance dates from "around 1291 or 1309, certainly 1315". The site uses 1291.
  • Neuchâtel: 1406 is used for the permanent alliance with Bern (seen in an HLS image caption only).
  • Valais: the 1416–1417 alliance with Lucerne, Uri and Unterwalden is not checked in the HLS text.
  • Glarus and Werdenberg: 1517 (HLS Werdenberg) vs. 1519 (HLS Glarus).
  • Glarus and Sargans: "about 1500"; the HLS Uri article says Sargans was a shared bailiwick from 1482.
  • Schwyz: Reichenburg 1803, 1814 or 1831; Gersau 1803, for good in 1817.
  • Fribourg: Payerne and Avenches went back to Vaud in 1801 or 1802 (two HLS articles disagree).
  • Vaud: 1476 for the start of shared rule over Grandson and Orbe-Echallens (Burgundian Wars). HLS gives no exact year.
  • Graubünden: no HLS date was found for the alliance of the Ten Jurisdictions with the Swiss.

Borders (drawn from today's units)

  • Bern 1353: the agent built the list from dated items. HLS has no list for this year.
  • Bern 1555: Saanen and the Pays-d'Enhaut are drawn as Bernese; the agent did not confirm this in HLS.
  • Basel 1501 and Solothurn 1393: the agents' own reading, mostly by district.
  • Zurich 1452: the bailiwicks are drawn as whole districts, so some villages may be dated too early.
  • Schaffhausen 1501/1530/1723: some purchase dates are unknown.
  • Graubünden: the three leagues are drawn by today's regions. The parts near Albula, Domleschg and Rhäzüns are approximate.
  • Geneva before 1815: the agent's own reconstruction.
  • Toggenburg 1798: the split between the cantons of Säntis and Linth is approximate.
  • Jura 1793: the north/south split follows today's border between JU and BE.
  • Lengnau, Pieterlen and Meinisberg (district Biel) are left out of old Bern.

Not confirmed in HLS

  • Rheinau as land shared by the cantons that ruled Thurgau.
  • Rapperswil's protecting cantons. Gams under Schwyz and Glarus (1497).
  • Laufental's 1989 vote. The 1831–1833 fights in Basel (only the split date, 26 August 1833, is confirmed).

Earlier rulers (round 2)

The maps before each canton existed are rougher than the later ones. Most rulers are drawn by whole districts.

North-east (earlier rulers)

  • ZH 1218: the Kyburg lands are drawn as the districts Winterthur and Pfäffikon, and the Regensberg lands as Dielsdorf. Both are rough guesses.
  • ZH 1300: Habsburg is drawn over the whole countryside. Many small lords are left out.
  • SH 1092: HLS gives "1080/1092" for the abbey's rule over the town.
  • SH 1330: the countryside is blank, except Neunkirch-Hallau (Bishop of Constance from 1302).
  • TG 1094 and 1264: all of Thurgau is drawn under Kyburg, then Habsburg, but abbeys and the bishop held land inside it. The bishop's rule over Arbon and Bischofszell in 1264 is a guess.
  • AR/AI 1300: all of today's Appenzell is drawn as abbey land. This is not confirmed for Trogen, Herisau and the Vorderland.
  • SG 1400: the year is approximate. The town's imperial status and Rapperswil under Habsburg are not confirmed.

Central Switzerland (earlier rulers)

  • Uri 1231: the year of Uri's freedom charter is the common date. HLS calls the charter disputed.
  • Schwyz, Obwalden, Nidwalden, Lucerne 1150: drawn as Lenzburg land. This is "about"; for Nidwalden it rests on later traditions.
  • The March in 1150 and 1240: "lords of Rapperswil and others (about)". Gersau and Arth before 1291 are not drawn.
  • Engelberg founding year 1120: from general knowledge.
  • Unterwalden 1257 and Lucerne 1291: drawn as Habsburg as a whole. No Kyburg period is drawn for Lucerne.
  • Zug: Habsburg in 1273 ("reportedly" in HLS).
  • Glarus: Kaltbrunn as part of Gaster is not confirmed. Otto of Burgundy (1173–1200) appears in the text only.

Graubünden and Ticino (earlier rulers)

  • GR 1365: drawn roughly with whole municipalities. Who held Tamins, Trin, Felsberg, the Gruob and the Werdenberg-Sargans land is a guess. Poschiavo and Brusio under Como/Milan before 1408 is "about".
  • GR 960: the bishop's land and the Disentis land are "about".
  • TI: the Leventina became Uri's for good in 1466 (HLS, Ticino campaigns) or in 1480 (HLS, canton article). The site uses 1480.
  • TI 1419: the 1403 alliance with the Leventina is drawn as Swiss control.
  • TI 1200: drawn by whole districts (Como, Empire).

North-west (earlier rulers)

  • Bern about 1200: the founding year 1191 is the traditional date. HLS says only "probably under Berthold V".
  • Solothurn 1250: the noble lands are drawn by today's districts. No date is known for when Solothurn became a free imperial town.
  • Basel 1260/1305: the Frohburg/Homberg land is drawn as the districts Liestal, Sissach and Waldenburg. The sale dates come from Museum.BL and the HLS Waldenburg article.
  • Aargau 1172: the Lenzburg land is drawn as the districts Lenzburg, Kulm and Baden. The Fricktal is drawn under the Homberg-Thierstein counts, and the date they got it is not checked.
  • Jura 999: the land of Moutier-Grandval abbey is unknown and is drawn as the Prévôté valleys. Jura 1283: the whole later Prince-Bishopric is drawn under the bishop.

West (earlier rulers)

  • Fribourg 1157: only the town is drawn as Zähringen land. Murten under Savoy "about 1255" is from the agent's memory.
  • Fribourg 1277: the Habsburg town is drawn with the later Old Countryside.
  • Vaud 1011: the whole canton is drawn as the bishop's. This is a simplification.
  • Vaud 1260: the bishop's land and the Grandson lords are drawn only roughly.
  • Valais 999: Saint-Maurice and Monthey are drawn as abbey land. Valais 1392: all of Martigny is drawn under Savoy.
  • Neuchâtel 1250: Valangin is drawn with mountain villages that were settled mostly later.
  • Geneva 1402: HLS gives 1401, 1402 and 1424 for Savoy getting the Genevois. The site uses 1402.

The Helvetic cantons 1798–1803

  • HLS is unclear about 1802. It says the 1802 constitution dissolved Waldstätten, Oberland, Baden, Bellinzona, Lugano, Linth and Säntis. But the articles on these cantons say that Baden, Linth, Säntis, Bellinzona and Lugano existed formally until 1803. The site keeps them in the 1802 maps.
  • Waldstätten was dissolved in "November 1801". HLS gives no day.
  • The Upper Toggenburg left Linth in 1801. No source says where it went, so it stays in Linth.
  • Borders drawn only roughly with today's municipalities: Oberland's part of the Thun district, Payerne/Avenches, Amt Hitzkirch (in Baden), Rüthi (in Linth), the Birseck and the Biel area. In April 1798 the planned eastern and central cantons are drawn only roughly.
  • Tarasp and Rhäzüns were Austrian until 1803. They cannot be drawn separately with today's municipalities.
  • From general knowledge, not checked in HLS: Geneva taken by French troops in April 1798 (treaty of 26 April 1798), and Ticino's capital rotating between Bellinzona, Locarno and Lugano.

Allies and protected places of the Old Confederation

The HLS website blocked direct downloads, so the agent read HLS only through summaries. All dates below should be checked on the HLS pages ("Zugewandte Orte" and the single articles).

  • Prince-Bishopric of Basel: alliance with the Catholic cantons 1579–1735 (one HLS article says it was renewed until 1717, and the bishop refused to renew it in 1735).
  • Moutier valley (Prévôté): HLS gives 1774 in one place and 1486–1798 in the table for Bern's protection. The site uses 1798.
  • Erguel, Moutier, La Neuveville, Tessenberg and Bellelay: France took them on 15 Dec 1797. The site ends them in 1798.
  • Places assigned without a source, municipality by municipality: Orvin and Pieterlen (Erguel), Rebévelier (Moutier), the Sous-les-Roches villages, the Birseck villages, Geneva's land before 1798 (partly inferred from the land added in 1815/16), and the Gruyère communes.
  • Valais: Hérens is drawn as Zenden land. The 1475 border of the Lower Valais is approximate. The Lötschental (ruled from 1376) is drawn from 1416.
  • Neuchâtel: older treaties exist (Fribourg 1290, Bern 1308, Solothurn 1369). Which cantons ruled it in 1512–1529 is not checked.
  • Rottweil: the alliance ends in 1632 on the map. Rottweil was excluded from the meetings in 1632, but the treaty was never ended.
  • Not drawn: Haldenstein (protected 1541–1798; today part of Chur), and the occasional allies around 1500 (the bishop of Konstanz, Württemberg, Montbéliard, Besançon, Arona).

Wars and battles (stories)

  • Areas are drawn with today's cantons, districts and regions, so they are rough: the Habsburg land of 1315–1405, the religion map, the 1653 rising, and the front lines of 1799. Burgundy is drawn as French départements plus Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg; Milan as Italian provinces; Ossola as the whole province of Verbano-Cusio-Ossola; the Swabian League as Baden-Württemberg plus Bavaria.
  • Battle sites: the Morgarten site is debated (the point is at the Schornen memorial). The points for Calven, Marignano (Zivido) and Neuenegg 1798 are approximate.
  • Dates: the Huttwil oath was on 4 or 14 May 1653 (old and new calendars). The day of Herzogenbuchsee is not checked. The Muotathal fights were about 30 Sept–1 Oct 1799, and the Panix crossing 5–7 Oct (HLS, Elm article: 5 October). Rothenthurm (2 May 1798) is not checked in HLS.
  • Numbers are estimates from HLS: St. Jakob an der Birs (about 1,200 Confederate and 2,000 Armagnac dead), Murten (8,000–20,000 Burgundian dead), Marignano (9,000–10,000 Swiss dead), Kappel (about 500 Zurich dead), Nidwalden 1798 (about 100 + 100 killed in battle, about 300 killed afterwards), Nancy (over 8,000 Swiss soldiers), Pavia (8,000 Swiss soldiers), Suvorov (about 20,000 men).
  • Not checked in HLS: the "milk soup" of Kappel 1529 (told as a story), Zurich mayor Stüssi's death at St. Jakob an der Sihl, Dufour's orders to treat prisoners humanely, the first Catholic-conservative Federal Councillor (1891), the date of the Pact of Brunnen (9 December 1315), and the rules of the Sempach Letter.

Lords before 1291 (stories)

  • Habsburg castle and Muri: HLS says only that Radbot or Wernher built the castle about 1020, and that Muri was founded "after 1020".
  • Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden in 1173: drawn under the Habsburg count rights in the western Zürichgau. This is only roughly right.
  • The Ewige Richtung (1474) is placed at Constance from general knowledge.
  • Early Savoy: HLS gives no date for Savoy taking the Chablais and Saint-Maurice ("in the 1000s"). Humbert's land is drawn very roughly. Peter II's protection of Bern and Murten ("in the 1250s") and his castles Yverdon and Chillon are not checked in HLS.
  • Kyburg: Hartmann IV's death in 1264 is inferred from HLS. The Kyburg split about 1250 is simplified. Several Kyburg town dates are only "later" or "about" in HLS.
  • The Rectorate of Burgundy (Zähringen) was a claim; its area is drawn very roughly.
  • Burgundy and Swabia about 950: Basel is counted as Burgundian, and the border is drawn roughly along the Aare.
  • Bishops in 1033: only Sion is confirmed in HLS as becoming a prince of the Empire.
  • Founding dates: St. Gallen and Disentis "in the 700s"; Engelberg 1120 and Einsiedeln 934 from general knowledge.
  • Habsburg in Europe (1282–1550): borders are rough. Lower Styria is left out in 1282; Slovenia and the Netherlands are drawn whole; Hungary and Croatia are left out; Milan is drawn as Lombard provinces plus Novara; the Breisgau is not drawn.
  • Geneva's countryside (1536–1720) is drawn roughly. Some battle points (Morgarten, Calven, Grandson, Murten, Payerne/Grindelwald 1190–1191) are approximate.

The Reformation (story)

  • Graubünden: the communes are read by eye from the Atlas Graubünden map "Konfessionsverhältnisse um 1640" (kulturforschung.ch, chapter 32). Unclear: one commune near Laax/Falera (both drawn Catholic), Trimmis (drawn mixed), and merged municipalities such as Ilanz/Glion, Domleschg, Cazis, Breil/Brigels and Obersaxen Mundaun (drawn mixed). Scuol is drawn Reformed, although Tarasp was Catholic and Austrian.
  • Thurgau, the Rheintal and Glarus are drawn mixed as a whole. HLS says Thurgau was mostly Reformed, with 27 shared churches; in the Rheintal only Oberriet is named as Catholic.
  • Toggenburg: HLS says only that the upper part was mostly Reformed and the lower part mostly Catholic. The split by municipality is an estimate.
  • County of Baden: shared parishes (Zurzach, Würenlos, Dietikon) and Reformed places (Tegerfelden, Schlieren, Oetwil, Hüttikon) come from general knowledge.
  • 1529: how far the Reformed faith spread in the common lordships, the abbey lands and Sargans is approximate. Gaster and Weesen are drawn Reformed only because HLS lists them in Zurich's 1531 blockade.
  • Echallens group: HLS supports Villars-le-Terroir, Bottens and Assens; Poliez-Pittet and Saint-Barthélemy are assumed. The year Orbe and Grandson became Reformed (1554) is not checked.
  • Laufen/Birseck drawn Catholic from 1597 (the bishop brought it back from the 1580s). Gex: Bernese 1536–1564, then mixed, then Catholic by 1685 (rough).
  • From memory, not checked: the papal nuncio in Lucerne from 1586, the Collegium Helveticum in Milan (1579), the Capuchins in Altdorf (1581), the Jesuit college in Fribourg (1582), and the Valtellina massacre (July 1620, starting at Tirano).
  • Calvin came to Geneva in June 1536 (HLS); other sources say July.
  • The Europe step is very rough: whole countries and German federal states only. France is drawn Catholic; its Reformed minority is in the text only.
